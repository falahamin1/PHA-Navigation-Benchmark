Headline result — GNN vs. Relational DeepSet, now that both exist at all 3 tiers

DESIGN.md §1.1's central claim is that GNN's advantage over set encoders should grow with incidence variability (EASY→MEDIUM→HARD), with encoder #5 (Relational DeepSet — H-Rep plus hand-designed adjacency features) as the "honest ablation": §1.8 frames a tie against it at HARD as the acceptable failure mode ("relational features are what matter"). With Relational's data now complete on every tier, the actual result is stronger than a tie — Relational DeepSet has the highest greedy solve rate of all 6 encoders at all 3 tiers, GNN included, and the gap is largest at EASY, not HARD (the opposite of the predicted shape).

Same shape holds for stochastic solve rate (not tabulated here — see the bar charts below, "stochastic" hoverable via the raw JSON). Two caveats worth weighing before treating this as final: (1) these are 5 seeds each under one frozen config (entropy_coef=0.01, no per-encoder tuning) — the frozen-config design was deliberate for fairness, but it means neither encoder got hyperparameter search; (2) MEDIUM's Relational CI is wide (16.8–41.2%) on 5 seeds, so that tier's overlap reflects real seed variance, not necessarily a weaker effect.

Physics-integrator confound — sim_violation rate in final greedy eval

A hard-coded assertion in nav_env.py (adjacency check on cell transitions) was firing and crashing training on HARD-tier jobs in the first batch — see callout above. It was patched to a caught, counted outcome (sim_violation, scored as not-solved) rather than a fixed dt/drift_coupling recalibration. That keeps training alive but means a slice of HARD-tier episodes are auto-scored as failures for a physics-engine reason, not a policy-quality reason. It's a small effect at final convergence (shown below) but not zero, and it lands unevenly across encoders. Now includes the previously-missing HARD H-Rep/Relational cells and V-Rep/GNN's remaining seeds.
